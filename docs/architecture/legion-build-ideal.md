## 1. Which loop this extends

**Place 4 — World map** (legions are how you go anywhere), **Place 3 — Farm, hunt, defend** (legions
hold, raid and escort), **Place 5 — World stage** (raising and outfitting happens in held sectors).
It feeds trade-network (caravans, crews) and world-continuity (wardens).

## 2. What this is

Today a legion is a list of creatures and a position. Raising one gives you a single fighter of a
species you do not choose. Legion building makes a legion something you **put together**: which units
go in and in what role, a **standard** it carries, **traditions** it earns in the field, one
**doctrine** that shapes how it fights and marches, and whether it runs on its own. A caravan is not a
separate thing — it is a legion you have told to run a trade route by itself.

## 3. Principles that constrain every choice below

1. **RPG layer only.** Legions fight through the one battle engine (`DistrictAssaultResolver` →
   `BattleEngine.Resolve`); nothing touches PvZ.
2. **One ActorHub compose.** Every number a legion changes on a creature enters through a registered
   `IActorStatSubsystem` or atom reader with a GG-49 SourceId. No `LegionComposer`, no private fold;
   `gk-core/scripts/guard-actor-hub.py` stays green.
3. **One battle engine.** A legion mode owns its **loop** (when a battle starts, who is placed where),
   never a **mechanism** (damage, targeting, status). Formation or cohesion effects are atoms, not
   engine forks.
4. **The actor layer stack is closed.** A new stat source is a layer that states scope, lifetime,
   carrier and provenance. Layer **5c legion** is approved (§8).
5. **General vs unique.** A legion's stacks are general creatures — no persistent instance, never N
   `UniqueActor` rows. General and unique creatures share **one progression** (owner, 2026-09-17); only
   equipment, then title, then later layers separate them. Uniques ride along through `InstanceId`.
   Stacks never get per-unit item rows (`docs/architecture/deployment-hierarchy-ideal.md:175`, *"HoMM-stack counts
   only"*) — their equipment is **legion equipment**, a counted stock of fixed-stat catalog pieces
   (§6.7).
6. **One legion, many behaviours.** Caravan, escort, garrison and warden are **orders and stances on
   one legion architecture**, never separate entity kinds or modes (owner ruling).
7. **Seed → concrete → per-player; the model writes identity only.** Standards, traditions and
   doctrines are generated as seeds; their magnitudes come from tables and `P(Θ)`.
8. **No player class; free build; no hard ceilings; one power ladder.**
9. **Capacity must not scale with the thing that burns it** (`empire-economy-ssot.md:217-218`).

## 6. The shape

### 6.1 What a legion is built from

| Block | What it is | Home | Layer / scope / lifetime |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stacks** | Species × **count** | A `Count` on `WorldEntityMember` | Layers 1a/1b (species) + **2b** (empire species progression) |
| **Attached uniques** | Roster specimens riding along | `InstanceId`, written by an `attach` act | Layer 2a + equipment, through `HubInputsFor` |
| **Commander** (optional) | A unique leading the legion; fights in siege and world assault (`docs/architecture/creature-system-map.md` amendment 2026-09-17) | A `Commander` member role | Existing commander aura path |
| **Roles** | Fighter, Bearer, Commander | Closed enum | Gate placement and eligibility (loop, not mechanism) |
| **Standard** | One per legion, carried by a named unit | A `world-buff` container bound to `OwnerKind.Legion` | **Layer 5c**; scope: that legion's members; lifetime: while its carrier lives and the legion exists |
| **Traditions** | Earned from the legion's own history (battles won, climates fought in, lanes held) | Rank-tiered atoms | Layer 5c; magnitude `P(Θ)` × tier from tuning |
| **Doctrine** | One exclusive pick per legion, with a world-side cost | A `world-buff` atom set + a signed modifier inside the **existing** pricers (`LaneCost`, `LegionSupply`) | Layer 5c for combat; never a second pricer |
| **Cohesion** | Mono-element legions gain, three or more elements lose | Read from composition (`BannerElement` spread) | Layer 5c atom |
| **Recruitment choice** | Raise picks a species from the sector's pool instead of a fixed one | A validated command parameter | — |
| **Legion equipment** | Fixed-stat pieces a stack wears, produced in sector buildings | A counted stock per piece; a stack's slots reference catalog ids (§6.7) | Layer 3 (equipment), **stack scope** — not a new layer |

Owner ruling (§8): **standards and traditions reset when a legion disbands or routs.** A legion's
identity is earned by keeping it alive.

### 6.2 Layer 5c — the one new stat source

Placed between empire title (5b) and aura (6). **Carrier:** a `world-buff` container. **Scope:** the
members of one legion (new closed `OwnerKind.Legion`, durable, unlike session-scoped `entity:`).
**Lifetime:** while the legion exists and, for a standard, while its carrier lives. **Provenance:** a
SourceId-registered atom reader. Contributions compose in `ActorHub` like every other layer.

### 6.3 Automation — standing orders on the one architecture

- A legion may hold a **standing order**: the same `WorldCommand` shape the AI already builds per
  legion (`FrontierRulesPolicy.cs:568-582`), stored against the legion.
- A per-legion standing-order resolver re-emits it each turn into the same command pipe every human
  and AI order uses, resuming mid-path where `MarchResolver` already resumes. Replay, hashing and the
  command log need no new cases.
- **With or without a commander** — automation reads the order, not the roster.
- Standing orders are also the owner-requested general automation the world-stage docs list as a real
  gap; this program supplies it.

### 6.4 Stances

Add **`escort`** (move with and screen another legion) to `march`/`scout`/`hold`. Stances are loop
behaviour; any combat effect of a stance is an atom.

### 6.5 Legions in trade and world continuity

- **Caravan = a legion on a trade standing order.** Its capacity comes from its bearers and cargo
  model; its cost is the loam burn and the action budget every legion pays.
- **Crews** for depots and hubs are bearers assigned from legions.
- **Escort** is a legion in the `escort` stance on the caravan's lane; its strength feeds the lane
  loss formula's `escortMilli`.
- **Interception** is an ordinary `Sector`/`Lane` battle between hostile legions — no special case.
- **Wardens** (world-continuity §6.4) are a commander and legion stationed on an old world.
- **Martial goods:** forging a standard and a doctrine's upkeep consume goods through the existing
  cargo and supply paths, so legion building creates trade demand.

### 6.7 Legion equipment — a new equipment scope (owner, 2026-09-19)

Owner ruling: *"a new type of equipment for legions … produced in buildings, not farmed from the lawn
or the delve … fixed stats, not generated like unique items (avoid generating a billion unique items)
… it still needs tier and fixed stats generated from seedsmith, but no effect-roll system … a new
equipment scope concept to avoid confusion … still has atom effects and set effects but no socket
system …"* — **amended the same day:** *"remove set items from legion items; sets stay with unique creature
items."* *"… weaker than unique creature items … a cheap and common version that can be massively crafted
through sector buildings."*

**Two equipment scopes, never confused:**

| | Unique-creature item (existing) | **Legion equipment (new)** |
|---|---|---|
| Worn by | One `UniqueActor` specimen | A general **stack** (species × count) |
| Identity | A rolled **instance** row per item | A **catalog piece** — no instance rows; a stack's slot names a piece id |
| Stats | Rolled per player at runtime (affixes, tier windows, rarity count bands) | **Fixed** per piece: tier and stats resolved deterministically from seedsmith seeds and tuning bands; identical for every player |
| Effects | Atom effects, sets, sockets, rolled affixes | **Atom effects only** — no sets (owner amendment, 2026-09-19), no sockets, no rolls |
| Power | The full item budget | **Weaker by design** — a tunable share of the unique-item budget at the same tier |
| Source | Drops from the lawn, delve and other loot tables; crafting | **Produced in sector buildings** only; recipes are learned from world-map events (a later extension) |
| Quantity | One item, one wearer | **A counted stock** in a sector warehouse; equipping a stack of *N* consumes *N* pieces |

- **Why a counted stock.** It keeps the stack rule (*"no per-unit item rows … HoMM-stack counts only"*)
  and the owner's "no billion unique items" in one shape: 3,000 pikes are one stock row, not 3,000 items.
  The cost of a turn does not grow with how much equipment exists.
- **Where the numbers enter.** Legion equipment contributes through the existing **equipment layer
  (3)** with a **stack scope** — a new scope and carrier on an existing layer, not a new layer — into
  `ActorHub` for the stack's members. The unique-item rules (sets, affix rolls, sockets, rarity count
  bands) never apply to this scope, and the item program's
  instance tables never hold it.
- **Production.** A sector building with a `refine`- ~~or `multiply`-~~role structure turns goods into
  pieces (the existing `workshop` row is the natural first home). *(Superseded in part: round 4 re-roles
  `workshop` from `Multiply` to `Refine` — goods in, pieces out is R2 — and makes it the Workshop → Armory
  → Foundry chain, `featureUnlock = legion-equipment`; production is gated through `sector-features`
  (round 5 X1), and pieces are held in the hashed sector warehouse, never `rpg_item_stock` (round 5 X4).)* Pieces are **located goods**: they sit
  in the producing sector's warehouse, travel by logistics, can be traded between empires, and are
  fitted to stacks where the legion stands.
- **Recipes** unlock from world-map events — the storylet engine's `story.flag` or a new closed outcome,
  designed when world-map events are extended.
- **Registry.** Legion equipment stock is a new quantity: it owes P4 (a real bottleneck — e.g. goods ×
  building time) and P6 (two competing sinks — fitting stacks and trade) and a row in
  `empire-resource-ssot.md` §3 in the change that adds it.
- **Wear and loss.** Whether pieces wear out, are lost when a stack takes casualties, or are captured
  with a routed legion is a spec-time rule; the default is that casualties consume the pieces their
  units wore (a sink that scales with war).

### 6.6 Retire `WorldEntityKind.Caravan`

It was never constructed. Caravans are legions; keeping the kind invites a second mode.

## 7. What empire-seed generates for this program

Identity only: standard seeds (`name`, `flavor`, `elementAffinity`, `atomFamilies[]`,
`carrierRequirement`, `forgeGoods[]`), tradition seeds (`triggerKind` from a closed list of world
facts, `atomFamily`, `rankNames[]`), doctrine seeds (`combatFamilies[]`, `worldTradeoffKind` ∈
march / burn / sight / cargo). **Cheap axis:** element × doctrine trade-off (6 × ~4 = 24 cells). Tests
assert the contract and closed enums, never how many standards exist.

## 10. Build order

Model-free wiring first: general members through layer 2b → role-aware placement → stack `Count` →
layer 5c binder and reader → standing orders and `escort` → recruitment choice → the remaining battle
kinds routed into the engine (the largest item; possibly its own module).

## 11. What this deliberately does not decide

Exact numbers; the fiction and names of standards and doctrines (empire-seed output); legion UI; how
many legions an empire may field (priced on a curve, never capped).
