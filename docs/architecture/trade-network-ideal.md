## 1. Which loop this extends

From `docs/guide/the-loops.md`:

- **Place 5 — World stage, empire building.** Trade centers, warehouses and depots sit in held
  sectors, next to slots, buildings, recruitment and upkeep. The primary home.
- **Place 4 — World map, adventure.** Goods move along lanes. Distance, hazard, fog and who holds the
  lanes between two points decide what a route is worth.
- **Place 3 — Farming, hunting, defending the empire.** *"Farm: hold ground, take sector yield."*
  Logistics is how that yield reaches a bank point; a route is something to hold, raid or protect.
- **Place 7 — Quests and events.** Trade produces the facts events react to: shortages, raids,
  clan requests, lost caravans (through the storylet engine `npc-story-events` owns).

World clock only — **virtual turns, End Turn.** No fourth clock. Nothing touches the lawn or PvZ.

## 2. What this is, in the player's language

Every held sector can build a **trade center** — a market with a **warehouse**. Your sectors produce
essence, shards and other goods; each sector's goods sit in its warehouse and **flow home on their own
every End Turn**, along the lanes that connect them to one of your **bank points** ~~(your capital, and
any vault you build)~~ *(superseded: a bank point is any sector with a **Counting House** — round 4 §B —
and every empire's seat starts with a tier-1 Counting House and Storehouse — round 5 A1,
[decisions-round-4.md](trade-network/decisions-round-4.md))*. Wide, warded lanes carry more and lose less. Long, hazardous lanes through
contested ground carry less and bleed goods to raids. When a warehouse is full, the sector stops
producing until the goods move.

Goods that reach a bank point are **banked** — they leave the map and join the wallet that carries
across worlds. Worlds do not end any more (world-continuity, 2026-09-19): a world you leave keeps going,
and goods still in its warehouses stay there — collected when you visit, by a legion's cargo, or by a
trade route between worlds.

Beyond your borders, **neutral clans** run trade centers of their own. An ice-marsh clan has ice
essence to spare and wants fire. You set a legion from one of your depots to run a trade route to them — every
exchange loses value, and their price climbs as you drain their stock — and the ice essence comes
back along the same lanes. Other empires trade at the same clan hubs, outbid you when they can, open or
close their own trade routes to you by treaty, and park legions on the lanes your goods travel.

Trade never makes you rich. It makes you **less stuck**, and it makes geography something you plan
around every turn.

## 3. Principles that constrain every choice below

Restated here because a downstream session reads this document, not its links.

1. **RPG layer only.** Trade and logistics are world-stage mechanics. They never touch PvZ, the
   injector or the lawn.
2. **Every faucet names its sink in the same change (P1).** The repo has paid for inflation once: an
   uncapped +2 souls per kill produced ~20–25 pulls an hour against a ~5–8 target.
3. **Territorial income needs territorial upkeep (P2).** *"Loam is the throttle on every faucet the
   map has"* (`empire-economy-ssot.md:197-198`). Every trade and logistics building pays loam upkeep.
4. **Any conversion between stocks must be lossy, rate-capped or gated (P5).** A free two-way exchange
   merges two stocks into one. Every conversion trade creates lands a row in the registry's
   Conversions column in the same change.
5. **Comparative advantage is the point (P12).** Element-typed essence is non-substitutable on purpose.
   Trade softens that pressure at a price; it never removes it.
6. **Loam is never traded or converted** (`docs/architecture/decisions.md:108`; `empire-resource-ssot.md`
   §4 rule 4). *"Every answer to a shortage is route, timing, capacity, risk"* (`empire-economy-ssot.md:209`).
7. **World stocks never feed an account-scoped path** (`empire-resource-ssot.md` §4 rule 5). Rubble,
   ironwork and recruits are map-scoped and never auto-bank; rubble and ironwork cross worlds only as
   legion cargo, recruits never (`empire-resource-ssot.md` §4 rule 5b; amended 2026-09-19, world-continuity).
8. **No sell price, no gold, souls never come out of trade** (`item/ssot-materials-crafting.md` §4.3).
   Trade may spend souls; it never pays them.
9. **The wallet has no location.** Banking is a **ledger fact** keyed by turn that takes goods off the
   map. Losing a sector, even the capital, never hands your banked treasury to anyone. *"You keep who
   you are"* (`docs/guide/the-game.md` §Win and lose; the old second half, *"you lose where you were"*,
   was retired 2026-09-19 by world-continuity — worlds persist).
10. **Every empire runs the same economy** (`empire-economy-ssot.md:124` states it for the enemy
    commander). Anything that throttles the player's goods throttles every AI empire's; a rule that
    binds only the player is a handicap and must be declared as one.
11. **One relation ladder.** Faction relations use the four-band disposition ladder
    `npc-story-events` defines (`docs/architecture/npc-story-events-ideal.md:417`) — derived from an
    append-only fact ledger, moved by facts, never by time. Trade does not add a second relation axis.
12. **One capacity axis per stock family.** The owner already rejected overloading one capacity field
    for two stocks (`gk-core/src/FusionRpg.Core/World/StructureCatalog.cs:40-42,74-78`). Warehouse goods get
    their own axis.
13. **Capacity must not scale with the thing that burns it** (`empire-economy-ssot.md:217-218`). A
    legion's carrying capacity comes from its bearers, never its headcount.
14. **Determinism (P13) and ledger before balance (P14).** Stocks, flows, prices and settlement are
    computed inside the world `step` from hashed state, in stable id order. Every mutation carries a
    dedupe key from a durable fact id.
15. **One power ladder; the balance surface is data; no hard ceilings.** Magnitudes read `ContentScale`
    / `P(Θ)` (`ssot-power-scale.md` §10 row 22). ~~Every number lives in `data/tuning/trade.v{n}.json`.~~
    *(Superseded — there is no single tuning file: each number lives in its owner's domain file —
    `trade.v{n}.json`, `diplomacy.v{n}.json`, `legion.v1.json` (proposed; the file does not exist yet) for legion mechanics and
    `legion-seed.v1.json` (proposed; the file does not exist yet) for legion seed magnitudes (round 4 Q12), `structure-seed` bands for building
    magnitudes (`empire-seed`); display words in `trade-catalog.v{n}.json`, never a number file.)*
    A limit on a magnitude is a soft cap; a per-turn rate is a structural limit and says so.
16. **No new currency without the registry gate** (P4, P6, a row in `empire-resource-ssot.md` §3).
17. **SOLID and one mechanism.** One Logistics phase in the one turn engine. Trade reuses the built
    movement cost, supply graph, valuation, storylet engine and structure corpus; it never forks them.
18. **Performance is a design constraint.** Nothing simulated per unit. Turn cost scales with graph
    size, never with cargo volume (§9).
19. **Generated content obeys the seed law** — seedsmith writes identity (names, enums), deterministic
    tables write magnitudes, the runtime rolls concrete objects. Detail in `empire-seed-ideal.md`.

## 6. Prior art (summary)

Four research passes, 2026-09-19. The lessons that shape this program are restated here. Numbers
marked *unverified* could not be confirmed from a primary source.

**Markets and prices.** Victoria 3 prices within 25%–175% of base using
`price = base × [1 + 0.75 × clamp((BUY−SELL)/min(BUY,SELL), ±1)]` ([wiki](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Market)).
Stellaris shipped an infinite-money loop because batches traded at one flat price
([forum](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/infinite-money-exploit-in-the-galactic-market.1135734/)).
Endless Legend's +25% buy / −60% sell spread makes round trips lose by construction
([wiki](https://endlesslegend.wiki.gg/wiki/Marketplace)). Civ's AI valuation leaked value through
buy/sell asymmetry and lump-sum-then-war deals
([source thread](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/ai-trade-values-gold-vs-gold-per-turn.483051/)).

**Logistics.** Shipped games that work put **pooled stock inside a node and aggregate flow between
nodes** (Anno's island pool, Victoria 3 convoys, HOI4 hubs). **Stellaris 4.0 removed its trade-route and
piracy layer**, citing *"a major impact on performance"*, with players calling it *"too micro
intensive"* (dev diary #369, search extract). Transport Fever 2's per-agent late-game lag is the
warning case. OpenTTD's rating spiral (neglect lowers rating, which lowers supply, which lowers rating)
and Anno's slot blocking (a full destination jams a ship) are the two failure shapes to design out.
Throughput = capacity ÷ cycle time (Factorio) is the abstraction that needs no vehicle.

**Performance.** Every studio that fixed a slow economy **reduced the number of simulated things**
(Victoria 3 pop merging, Stellaris 4.0 pop groups, one market instead of pairwise routes). OpenTTD runs
routing as a bounded background job with two approximate flow passes
([linkgraph.md](https://github.com/OpenTTD/OpenTTD/blob/master/docs/linkgraph.md)). Factorio's belt
rewrite made item movement 50–100× faster by storing gaps, not positions
([FFF #176](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-176)); multithreading its electric network raised CPU
0.5% → 15% with no speedup ([FFF #421](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-421)).

**Content and events** (detail in `empire-seed-ideal.md`). Anno 1800 production buildings each make
exactly one good and never take more than three inputs; Against the Storm makes 76% of goods producible
in two or more workshops. Stellaris ships 5,307 events, 92% fired by something else and 67% of visible
ones single-option notices; its modding wiki says MTTH polling *"has fallen out of favor"* for
performance. EU4 added a size-scaling flag against *"event spam"* in large countries.

**Lessons carried into §7–§9:**

1. Pooled stock per sector; aggregate flow per lane; vehicles are capacity, not agents.
2. Price an order along its own curve; spread wider than any single-turn swing; clamp to a band.
3. Quote at the start of the step, settle at the end.
4. Every fee, spread, waste and upkeep is a sink.
5. Publish every logistics formula to the player; show per-lane flow and the bottleneck reason.
6. No rating spiral; no refused unload.
7. Risk is a lane number suppressed by protection, never spawned pirate units.
8. Automate the flow; the player sets policy.
9. One valuation function for both sides of every AI deal.
10. Guarantee interdependence: production split across climates; counterparties with their own demand.
11. Recompute on change, bound every iteration, count nothing per unit.

## 7. The shape — trade

### 7.1 The trade center is a structure

A trade center is a **row in the structure corpus** (`gk-data/packs/fusion/data/seed/structures/`), built on a buildable
slot through the built `BuildResolver`, not a special case of the Market slot. The Market slot stays as
the preferred site (a trade center there gets a bonus), *(round 5 B1: the one Trading Post row allows
`Wildland` or `Market` through a multi-kind `requiredSlotKinds` field)* and the two shipped templates are edited so
every sector has a buildable site for one; the world generator gets the same rule when it lands. This
closes the Market-slot wiring gap without making Market the only place trade can happen.

Developing a center raises its **clearing capacity** (units exchanged per turn), a per-turn structural
rate. The center pays loam upkeep through a new structure term in `LoamUpkeep` (principle 3).

### 7.2 Prices and valuation

Each center keeps, per good, a **local stock** (its warehouse) and a **local demand** from its owner's
needs and its sector's climate. That demand is the number `INeedVector` is missing today, so a center's
demand and the AI's needs are **one SSOT**. The same valuation is the one the delve merchant and the
item program need; `exchange` owns it and both consume it.

```
imbalance = clamp((demand − stock) / min(demand, stock), −1, +1)
price     = base × (1000 + bandMilli × imbalance / 1000) / 1000     // e.g. 25%..175% of base
buy       = price × (1000 + spreadBuyMilli)  / 1000
sell      = price × (1000 − spreadSellMilli) / 1000
```

- An order walks the price every `orderStepUnits`; a batch never trades at one price.
- Quotes are taken at the start of the step and settled at its end, in `(CommanderId, CommandId)` order.
- **Only demand drifts back toward baseline.** A clan's **stock** comes from its own sector production,
  under the same rules as everyone's (corrects §10 C4) — stock that refills from nothing would mint
  banked goods every turn.
- `base` is a value index, not a currency. Nobody holds it.

### 7.3 What can be traded

| Good | May trade? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `essence.{element}` | **Yes — the headline good** | The P12 good |
| `shard.*`, `substrate.*` | Yes | Banked materials; the spread keeps them non-substitutable |

Testable invariants: **no trade path increases souls; no trade path moves a world stock into a banked
stock; buying then selling the same quantity at the same center always loses value.**

### 7.4 Counterparties

- **Your own centers** — moving your own goods is logistics, not trade (decision 19): no price, no
  spread.
- **Neutral clans and allied enclaves** — the main partners (D3). A clan sector produces goods under
  the same production rules as yours. Its personality — the goods it craves and pays a premium for — is
  **computed from climate and species**, never authored per clan (`world-graph-ideal.md` §8.2).
  Relation uses the **one four-band ladder** from `npc-story-events` (principle 11), and scales the
  spread. **Conquest is already a designed option** (`world-graph-ideal.md` §8.2: Conquest / Contract /
  Ignore); capturing a clan sector closes its center, ends clan production there, and records a fact
  that moves every clan's disposition.
- **Other empires** — a world has **many empires** (owner, 2026-09-19), seeded as `Rival`-kind
  factions with policy ids for personality. Each runs the same goods economy (principle 10): its
  sectors produce goods; it spends them on **world-scoped sinks** (building costs, legion upkeep,
  siege preparation); it holds a **per-world treasury** (world-scoped, hashed). You trade with an empire
  **only through the routes it opens to you by treaty** (§7.7) — never by a hard-coded rule.

### 7.5 The AI uses the same machinery

AI empires and clans file the same route and order commands; offers are scored by `ValueMap` with real
needs — **one valuation function for both sides**. No lump-sum-for-per-turn deals in v1. A per-turn AI
purchasing limit is a structural limit, stated in a comment.

### 7.6 The deal lifecycle and transit rights

Answering `empire-development-map.md` open question #6:

- **Risk transfers at the seller's center.** Goods in transit belong to the buyer.
- **Partial fills** are normal: an order fills what the center's stock and clearing capacity allow;
  the remainder stays open until its policy says otherwise.
- **Cancelling** stops future fills; goods already in transit keep travelling.
- **A counterparty captured mid-route** — goods not yet delivered follow the same fate rules as cargo
  on capture (`cargo-fate`, `scoped-inventory` program).
- **Transit rights** — a route may cross another empire's ground only under a `passage` treaty (§7.7);
  your own and unheld ground is always open. Crossing hostile ground needs a legion on a trade standing
  order that can fight its way through (§8.3).
- **Who trades at your hub** — whoever your treaties and relation bands allow (§7.7); access is derived
  every turn, never stored as a separate permission list.

### 7.7 Diplomacy and treaties (round 3)

Owner ruling (2026-09-19): trade between empires is **not hard-coded** — *"we can trade with empires if
they open their trading route for us"* — learned from Endless Space and the real world.

- **Access is derived, never stored.** `Access(grantor, requester, turn)` ∈ {closed, passage, market,
  preferential} is a pure function of (a) treaty facts in the ledger for that pair, (b) the relation
  band from the one four-band ladder, and (c) an active embargo. Replaying the ledger rebuilds it.
- **A closed treaty vocabulary** (one registry, `treaty-kind.v1`):

  | Kind | Scope | Grants | Lowest band |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | `passage` | bilateral | Your legions and routes may cross the grantor's sectors (transit rights) | wary |
  | `market` | bilateral | Orders at the grantor's hubs; its lanes join your route graph (implies `passage`) | open |
  | `preferential` | bilateral | Lower tariff, with a most-favoured clause: never above the lowest tariff the grantor charges anyone | eager |
  | `bloc` | multilateral — **in v1** (owner, round 3) | No tariffs inside; one shared external tariff and shared embargoes; members give up setting their own | eager with every member |
  | `embargo` | one-sided act | Stops the target's orders and passage; voids `market` and `preferential` | any band; **automatic at war** |

- **The band limits what is possible; valuation decides what happens.** An empire signs when the one
  valuation function (`ValueMap` + real `INeedVector` needs) says the treaty gains it at least
  `acceptMilli[band]`. The same function scores player proposals, AI-to-AI proposals and the AI's own
  decision to open or close routes. Counter-offers come from a fixed article list, never a solver
  (Civ's "make it equitable" exploit). Every refusal shows its terms.
- **Tariffs are a sink.** Charged on the value index at settlement; `tariff.sinkMilli` is destroyed and
  the rest goes to the grantor in goods. **Any souls part of a tariff is sunk in full**, so no trade
  path ever increases souls.
- **Events move access as ledger facts:** `war.declared` voids treaties and embargoes both ways;
  `treaty.broken` (ended before its minimum term) drops the breaker's band with the partner and, less,
  with every observer holding a treaty with it, then a truce period; `trade.fulfilled` writes a small
  positive relation fact, **capped per pair per turn** (owner, round 3); a peace term may impose
  `market` as `treaty.imposed`.
- **Blockers:** real `INeedVector` needs must exist first — a uniform-needs AI would accept everything.
  `spec-ai-commander.md:284` (*"no diplomacy"*) is amended for this program; `empire-development-map.md`
  open question #6 is answered by this flow.
- **Payment** is barter at the value index, topped up with souls (owner, round 3). Souls → goods is a new
  lossy conversion row in the registry. Invariant: **any closed trade cycle across any number of hubs in
  one turn loses value.**

## 8. The shape — logistics

### 8.1 Abstraction level (D5)

| Noun | In the simulation | Not |
|---|---|---|
| **Warehouse** | One pooled stock per (sector, good) | Crates, slots |
| **Lane flow** | Units per turn along a lane, bounded by the lane | Moving vehicles |
| **Transport** | A legion's bearer capacity on a trade standing order | A pathing agent per crate |
| **Caravan** | An ordinary legion running a trade route automatically, with or without a commander | A separate entity kind or mode |

Turn cost scales with **sectors × goods + routes × goods × transit turns**, never with cargo volume.

### 8.2 Warehouse

- One warehouse per trade center, pooled across the sector.
- **Its own capacity axis** (principle 12), deliberately separate from loam's `Storage` capacity and
  from Data-side `ItemStorage`, which is unhashed. Warehouse stock is hashed world state.
- **Production halts at capacity** — the shipped decision-22 rule for construction stocks
  (`gk-core/src/FusionRpg.Core/World/StructurePolicy.cs:56-66`), not waste. **Only incoming deliveries** that
  arrive at a full warehouse overflow and waste a fraction per turn. The player is notified in both
  cases.
- A captured sector's warehouse changes hands with the sector. **Banked goods are never in a
  warehouse** (principle 9), so capture can take only what had not yet reached a bank point.

### 8.3 Transport: caravans are legions (round 3)

Owner ruling (2026-09-19): *"A caravan is an automatic legion, with or without a commander. We don't
need to split legions into multiple modes; we need to extend its architecture."* This replaces the
previous draft's hauler catalog and its `WorldEntityKind.Caravan`.

- **Banking does not need transport.** Lanes alone bound the flow of yields to bank points (§10 C11).
- **Foreign and cross-world routes are run by legions on a trade standing order** — the per-legion
  automation `legion-build` adds (`legion-build-ideal.md` §6.3). Capacity comes from the legion's
  bearers and its cargo model; throughput = trips × capacity ÷ round-trip turns.
- **Depots** ~~(the `convoy-depot` row)~~ *(superseded: the `caravan-yard` row, Caravan Yard → Convoy
  Depot, where `convoy-depot` is its tier-2 variant — round 5 X12)* are where trade legions load and unload
  and where route range starts. *(Round 5 B3: a caravan unloads at a **foreign** hub only when the hub's
  owner has a Caravan Yard in that sector.)*
- **Crews are units.** Hubs and depots need assigned bearers to run — labour is both a production input
  and an upkeep line (P11), and it gives the never-produced Bearer role a job. *(Round 5 X9: `fleet`
  `crew` supplies the bearer count; `exchange` owns the hub staffing term and its key.)*
- **Upkeep, without double charging.** Buildings pay loam through the structure upkeep term. A trade
  legion pays what every legion pays: loam burn and the action budget.
- **Interception and escort are ordinary legion play.** A hostile legion meeting a caravan triggers the
  kind-agnostic `Sector`/`Lane` battle; an escort is a legion in the `escort` stance. No special case.
- **`WorldEntityKind.Caravan` is retired.**

### 8.4 Lanes and routes

- **`Width` becomes throughput.** Flow on a lane per turn is `min(demand on the lane, Width ×
  throughputPerWidth)`, shared by route priority, then by stable id. A **road-widening verb** raises
  `Width`, so throughput is something you build, not a template constant — without it, `Width` would be
  a hidden ceiling.
- **`WardLevel` keeps its siege meaning and gains a logistics one.** It already sets siege approach
  depth (`DistrictLayout.cs:342-350`); it now also suppresses lane loss. The two roles point the same
  way (a warded lane is a defended lane), so one field serves both — stated explicitly so no later
  program splits it by accident. The unbuilt **`ward` verb** raises it.
- **Loss** per lane per turn is
  `goods × max(0, hazardMilli + hostilePresenceMilli − WardLevel × protectionPerWard − escortMilli) / 1000`
  — a deterministic fraction, no spawned raiders.
- **Transit time** is `ceil(path LaneCost ÷ speed)`; each route keeps a short in-transit buffer, so a
  cut lane strands what is on it. `deep` and `one-way` lanes carry no supply today
  (`LaneTypeCatalog.cs:61-62`); whether yields may bank across them is a flow rule the spec states.
- **Paths are cached** and recomputed only when the graph changes.
- **No rating spiral** — neglect never lowers the supply a route needs to recover.

| **Bank points** | ~~The capital and any vault-role structure~~ *(superseded: any sector with a Counting House, round 4 §B; every seat starts with one, round 5 A1)*; flows are not a star through one capital | ~~`vault` slot, `bank` structure role~~ *(superseded: the `counting-house` row, `featureUnlock = banking`, read through `sector-features`; the `bank` role keeps its currency-source meaning)* |

### 8.6 Shape B, as corrected

Yields land in the producing sector's warehouse and **flow automatically toward the nearest bank point
along connected lanes**, bounded by lane throughput and subject to lane loss. On arrival, a **ledger
fact keyed by `(world_id, turn, sector, good)`** takes the goods off the map into the unlocated wallet
(principle 9). Worlds no longer end (world-continuity, round 3): a world you leave hibernates, its
warehouses keep their goods, and they are collected on a visit, by cargo, or by a cross-world route.

Consequences worth naming:

- **Shape B is cheaper than the previous draft said.** No shipped code banks sector yields today
  (§5 real gap), so there is no existing income to re-time. It is built this way from the start.
- **It cannot come first.** The reward layer that produces goods is the first thing to build.
- **Income parity.** Income with a location — a delve door, a battle sector — lands in that sector's
  warehouse and travels like any yield. Expedition hauls, which have no world location, bank as today.
- **Teaching.** On a player's first world, lanes never bind; logistics is taught at the first throttle.
  Route orders and clans unlock at first clan contact or at the empire-development milestone.

### 8.7 Where it runs

- **One new phase, `Logistics`**, after Production and before Growth: production halts, lane flow,
  arrivals and banking facts, loss, market quote and settlement. Everything that changes a hashed stock
  runs inside `step`.
- **New command kinds** `route-set`, `route-clear`, `order-set`, `caravan-send`, `ward`, `widen`,
  admitted through `WorldCommandAdmission`. Policy commands set policy; they move nothing themselves.
- **Ledger rows** per delivery, banking fact and settled exchange, deduped on a durable fact id.

### 9.2 Engineering rules

1. **Measure first** — a `TurnEngine.Step` benchmark at medium and giant tiers lands before the
   Logistics phase, timed through a `PerfProbe`-style facade.
2. **Nothing per unit.**
3. **Paths recompute on graph change only;** never reuse the O(V⁴) sweep for routing.
4. **Bounded work** — one flow pass in priority order plus at most one redistribution pass; never an
   exact multi-commodity solve.
5. **Dirty sets** — only changed centers re-quote.
6. **Integer or fixed-point for hashed state; stable iteration order** (floating point is allowed by
   repo ruling, but a hashed double needs a platform stamp).
7. **Flat arrays, zero allocation in the Logistics phase after warm-up**, asserted by a benchmark.
8. **Persistence proportional to change** — goods stock rides the diff writer; the spec decides row per
   good versus one packed row per sector.

**Economy and systems**

| Gap | Closed by |
