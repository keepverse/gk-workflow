# The game

**Status:** concept — the fiction holds whether a surface is Shipped, WIP, or Vision.

This page and [The loops](the-loops.md) are the **product vision** for Garden Keeper and his Multiverse. Every feature in this guide hangs on that vision.

---

## What this is

**Garden Keeper and his Multiverse is an RPG plus empire-building extension for Fusion.**

It stays inside Fusion’s own world — its plants and zombies — and grows it into a persistent war. Its leads are the game’s own: the Garden Keeper and Hourbloom, against the Rotwright. You raise a creature roster, collect and progress gear, grow the Garden Keeper’s power, and run an empire across a fractured multiverse. Idle expeditions run while you play. The **lawn** is the **first core loop** — what you play first, and what feeds almost everything — not the whole war, and not optional flavor.

You need a legal install of **Fusion** (a fan-made pack of the host game, separate from EA’s official titles). Garden Keeper and his Multiverse sits on top of it — it does not replace the lawn game.

You are **the Garden Keeper**. Your capital is his **homeworld** — the one timeline still yours. Creatures are what the Fracture made of your lawn’s plants and zombies. Hourbloom is how legions reach the other eras at all.

On a fresh Sanctum entry, a short optional [Rift prologue](../ideas/onboarding-gnome-teaser.md) frames
the fiction before the first lawn. It is a presentation-only warning: it does not create a save slot,
grant a reward, or replace the lawn-first progression sequence.

Everything you own lives at home: roster, reserve, altar, fusion lab, almanac, stored haul. That is why defending the homeworld is the spine, not one more objective.

## How we got here

The Rotwright’s time machine malfunctioned and scattered its pieces across time and space. He sent zombies through the eras to recover them; the Garden Keeper chased him with Hourbloom. Where a shard landed, plant and zombie **fused** — that is how creatures entered the war.

You chase him through the **rift** — a network of sectors joined by lanes — and you fight on ground that can fade if you neglect it.

## Win and lose

| | |
|---|---|
| **Win** | Take the seat of the world’s dominant enemy empire. The world does not end: it becomes *developing*, keeps running, and the other enemy empires keep acting |
| **Lose** | Enemy empires take your seat. That world becomes *fallen* — hostile ground you can revisit — and your save continues |

**You keep who you are. The worlds you leave keep going.**

Roster, souls, and essence bank across worlds. Loam never leaves the world it was made on. You may **advance** to a new world at any time, carrying what your legions can carry — more after a win. The worlds you leave *hibernate* or, with a warden stationed, go *idle*; they keep their enemy pressure and upkeep, and an unguarded one can fall. Advancing *is* the progression loop — not a soft reset of your summoner.

## How the war is played

You start on the **lawn**. You raise creatures. You send spare creatures on **expeditions**. When you are ready, you step onto the **rift**: adventure on the map, empire on the world stage, crawls in delves, quests and events tying it together.

The control room runs on your machine. Unlocked web features stay playable with the **lawn game** closed once you have opened them — nothing essential stays lawn-only forever. The lawn still feeds almost every spine loop: souls, levels, almanac, deploy, later capture and blessing.

Same roster. Same souls. Same world. Whichever place you are playing from.

The full list of loops — spine and places — lives on [The loops](the-loops.md). That page is what later features must extend.

---

## The loop in one breath

> Play the lawn → grow power, summon, and gear → dispatch idle expeditions → farm, hunt, and defend the empire → adventure the map and build the stage → delve and quest → do it again on ground that fights back.

Every page in this guide is a piece of that.

---

## What you are not

- You are not a class. Character building is free — points go where you want.
- You are not running a cloud MMO. Play is local, on your machine, with no account.
- You are not patching the game binary. The lawn game stays a legal Fusion install you point the launcher at.
- You are not juggling a wallet of interchangeable currencies. **Souls** and **essence** bank across worlds; **loam**, **rubble**, **ironwork**, and **recruits** belong to the world they were made on and never bank. Rubble and ironwork can cross to another world only as a legion’s cargo; loam and recruits never cross. Each one buys something nothing else can. There is no stamina gate.
